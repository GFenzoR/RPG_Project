A pixel-art animation sprite sheet of one creature for a dark fantasy top-down role-playing game: an ore beetle of the harbour island mine, a beetle as big as a dog. A heavy domed shell of dark iron grey with green sheens, crusted with lumps of rusty brown iron ore and rock, six short sturdy dark legs, a broad low head with two thick crushing mandibles.

Eight rows of frames, each row a separate strip, every frame the same beetle at the same scale on the same ground line. The camera always looks down at it from about 45 degrees, so its top shows in every view:
- Row 1, four frames: the beetle crawling toward the camera, seen from above and in front (we see its head, mandibles and the front of its shell, with its domed back above them; the legs alternate).
- Row 2, four frames: the beetle crawling away from the camera, seen from above and behind (we see the back of its domed shell with the ore crust and its legs; the legs alternate).
- Row 3, four frames: the beetle crawling to the right, in profile facing right.
- Row 4, six frames: the beetle standing still, mandibles working: two frames facing the camera, two frames seen from behind, two frames in profile facing right.
- Row 5, three frames: the beetle biting toward the camera, seen from above and in front (rears its head, snaps its mandibles shut, settles back).
- Row 6, three frames: the beetle biting away from the camera, seen from above and behind (rears its head, snaps its mandibles shut, settles back).
- Row 7, three frames: the beetle biting to the right, in profile facing right (rears its head, snaps its mandibles shut, settles back).
- Row 8, three frames: the beetle dying, seen from the front (flips onto its back, legs twitching, then lies still).

The same silhouette, proportions, colours and size in every frame: only the pose and the facing change.

Hard technical requirements, these matter more than the art direction:
- Solid flat magenta background, exactly RGB(255, 0, 255), filling every pixel that is
  not part of a drawing. No gradient, no vignette, no texture, no shadow cast onto it.
- Lay the subjects out in an even grid with equal spacing. Every cell the same size.
  No labels, no captions, no text of any kind anywhere in the image.
- Every subject fully inside its own cell, centred, touching neither its neighbours nor
  the edge of the image. In an animation, the same size and the same ground line in
  every cell.
- Three-quarter top-down view: the camera looks down from the south at about 45
  degrees, so the top and the south face of things show. Light from the top left.
- Pixel art: crisp square pixels, hard edges, no anti-aliasing, no blur, no soft
  airbrush shading. Limited palette, flat shading in three or four tones, and a dark
  outline of the local colour (never pure black).
- Dark fantasy colours: muted and earthy, with one brighter accent at most. Nothing in
  a subject may be magenta, pink, purple or violet: those hues are the background key.
- They are read at 32x32 pixels in game (people at 64x64), so favour a strong
  silhouette and clear separation of parts over fine detail.
