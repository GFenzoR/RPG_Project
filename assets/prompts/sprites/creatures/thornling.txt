A pixel-art animation sprite sheet of one creature for a dark fantasy top-down role-playing game: a thornling of the deep forest, a walking bramble creature as tall as a man. A body of twisted dark brown wood and thorny vines, green moss and a few leaves on its shoulders, small glowing yellow-green eyes in a knotted face, long whip-like thorny arms, legs like roots.

Eight rows of frames, each row a separate strip, every frame the same thornling at the same scale on the same ground line. The camera always looks down at it from about 45 degrees, so its top shows in every view:
- Row 1, four frames: the thornling lumbering toward the camera, seen from above and in front (we see its knotted face, chest and thorny arms, with its back above them; the root legs alternate).
- Row 2, four frames: the thornling lumbering away from the camera, seen from above and behind (we see its back of twisted wood and vines; the root legs alternate).
- Row 3, four frames: the thornling lumbering to the right, in profile facing right.
- Row 4, six frames: the thornling standing still, swaying and creaking: two frames facing the camera, two frames seen from behind, two frames in profile facing right.
- Row 5, three frames: the thornling lashing with its thorny arm toward the camera, seen from above and in front (draws its arm back, lashes it down, recovers).
- Row 6, three frames: the thornling lashing with its thorny arm away from the camera, seen from above and behind (draws its arm back, lashes it down, recovers).
- Row 7, three frames: the thornling lashing with its thorny arm to the right, in profile facing right (draws its arm back, lashes it down, recovers).
- Row 8, three frames: the thornling dying, seen from the front (splinters, sags to its knees, collapses into a heap of broken branches and thorns).

The same silhouette, proportions, colours and size in every frame: only the pose and the facing change.

Hard technical requirements, these matter more than the art direction:
- Solid flat magenta background, exactly RGB(255, 0, 255), filling every pixel that is
  not part of a drawing. No gradient, no vignette, no texture, no shadow cast onto it.
- Lay the subjects out in an even grid with equal spacing. Every cell the same size.
  No labels, no captions, no text of any kind anywhere in the image.
- Every subject fully inside its own cell, centred, touching neither its neighbours nor
  the edge of the image. In an animation, the same size and the same ground line in
  every cell.
- Three-quarter top-down view: the camera looks down from the south at about 45
  degrees, so the top and the south face of things show. Light from the top left.
- Pixel art: crisp square pixels, hard edges, no anti-aliasing, no blur, no soft
  airbrush shading. Limited palette, flat shading in three or four tones, and a dark
  outline of the local colour (never pure black).
- Dark fantasy colours: muted and earthy, with one brighter accent at most. Nothing in
  a subject may be magenta, pink, purple or violet: those hues are the background key.
- They are read at 32x32 pixels in game (people at 64x64), so favour a strong
  silhouette and clear separation of parts over fine detail.
