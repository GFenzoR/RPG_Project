A pixel-art animation sprite sheet of one creature for a dark fantasy top-down role-playing game: a moss spider of the coast woods, a hunting spider as big as a dog. A round body overgrown with green moss and grey-green lichen, eight long jointed legs banded dark brown and olive, a cluster of small glossy black eyes, curved fangs wet with green sap.

Eight rows of frames, each row a separate strip, every frame the same spider at the same scale on the same ground line. The camera always looks down at it from about 45 degrees, so its top shows in every view:
- Row 1, four frames: the spider scuttling toward the camera, seen from above and in front (we see its eyes, fangs and front legs, with its mossy body above them, with its back above them; the legs alternate).
- Row 2, four frames: the spider scuttling away from the camera, seen from above and behind (we see its mossy abdomen and back legs; the legs alternate).
- Row 3, four frames: the spider scuttling to the right, in profile facing right.
- Row 4, six frames: the spider crouched, breathing, its legs shifting: two frames facing the camera, two frames seen from behind, two frames in profile facing right.
- Row 5, three frames: the spider striking with its fangs toward the camera, seen from above and in front (rears up on its back legs, strikes down with its fangs, settles).
- Row 6, three frames: the spider striking with its fangs away from the camera, seen from above and behind (rears up on its back legs, strikes down with its fangs, settles).
- Row 7, three frames: the spider striking with its fangs to the right, in profile facing right (rears up on its back legs, strikes down with its fangs, settles).
- Row 8, three frames: the spider dying, seen from the front (curls its legs in, rolls onto its back, lies still).

The same silhouette, proportions, colours and size in every frame: only the pose and the facing change.

Hard technical requirements, these matter more than the art direction:
- Solid flat magenta background, exactly RGB(255, 0, 255), filling every pixel that is
  not part of a drawing. No gradient, no vignette, no texture, no shadow cast onto it.
- Lay the subjects out in an even grid with equal spacing. Every cell the same size.
  No labels, no captions, no text of any kind anywhere in the image.
- Every subject fully inside its own cell, centred, touching neither its neighbours nor
  the edge of the image. In an animation, the same size and the same ground line in
  every cell.
- Three-quarter top-down view: the camera looks down from the south at about 45
  degrees, so the top and the south face of things show. Light from the top left.
- Pixel art: crisp square pixels, hard edges, no anti-aliasing, no blur, no soft
  airbrush shading. Limited palette, flat shading in three or four tones, and a dark
  outline of the local colour (never pure black).
- Dark fantasy colours: muted and earthy, with one brighter accent at most. Nothing in
  a subject may be magenta, pink, purple or violet: those hues are the background key.
- They are read at 32x32 pixels in game (people at 64x64), so favour a strong
  silhouette and clear separation of parts over fine detail.
