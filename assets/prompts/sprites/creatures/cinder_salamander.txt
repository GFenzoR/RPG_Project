A pixel-art animation sprite sheet of one creature for a dark fantasy top-down role-playing game: a cinder salamander of the volcanic island's lava pools, a salamander as long as a man is tall. A long, low body on four short splayed legs, rough charcoal-grey skin covered in deep red scales, a row of glowing orange cracks along its back, a long thick tail, a broad flat head with small yellow eyes. It lives in the lava and spits burning slag.

Eight rows of frames, each row a separate strip, every frame the same salamander at the same scale on the same ground line. The camera always looks down at it from about 45 degrees, so its top shows in every view:
- Row 1, four frames: the salamander crawling toward the camera, seen from above and in front (we see its broad head and front legs, with its back and tail behind them; the legs alternate and the body sways).
- Row 2, four frames: the salamander crawling away from the camera, seen from above and behind (we see its back with the glowing cracks and its tail; the legs alternate and the tail sways).
- Row 3, four frames: the salamander crawling to the right, in profile facing right.
- Row 4, six frames: the salamander standing still, its throat pulsing: two frames facing the camera, two frames seen from behind, two frames in profile facing right.
- Row 5, three frames: the salamander lunging toward the camera, seen from above and in front (rears its head, lunges with open jaws glowing inside, settles back).
- Row 6, three frames: the salamander lunging away from the camera, seen from above and behind (rears its head, lunges with open jaws, settles back).
- Row 7, three frames: the salamander lunging to the right, in profile facing right (rears its head, lunges with open jaws glowing inside, settles back).
- Row 8, three frames: the salamander dying, seen from the front (writhes, rolls onto its side, lies still with its glow gone dark).

The same silhouette, proportions, colours and size in every frame: only the pose and the facing change.

Hard technical requirements, these matter more than the art direction:
- Solid flat magenta background, exactly RGB(255, 0, 255), filling every pixel that is
  not part of a drawing. No gradient, no vignette, no texture, no shadow cast onto it.
- Lay the subjects out in an even grid with equal spacing. Every cell the same size.
  No labels, no captions, no text of any kind anywhere in the image.
- Every subject fully inside its own cell, centred, touching neither its neighbours nor
  the edge of the image. In an animation, the same size and the same ground line in
  every cell.
- Three-quarter top-down view: the camera looks down from the south at about 45
  degrees, so the top and the south face of things show. Light from the top left.
- Pixel art: crisp square pixels, hard edges, no anti-aliasing, no blur, no soft
  airbrush shading. Limited palette, flat shading in three or four tones, and a dark
  outline of the local colour (never pure black).
- Dark fantasy colours: muted and earthy, with one brighter accent at most. Nothing in
  a subject may be magenta, pink, purple or violet: those hues are the background key.
- They are read at 32x32 pixels in game (people at 64x64), so favour a strong
  silhouette and clear separation of parts over fine detail.
