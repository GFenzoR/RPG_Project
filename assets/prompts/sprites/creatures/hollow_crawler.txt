A pixel-art animation sprite sheet of one creature for a dark fantasy top-down role-playing game: a hollow crawler of the smugglers' cave under the forest island shore, a blind cave insect as long as a man is tall. A long, low body of many segments armoured in pale, light bone-white chitin plates with dark grey-brown joints, a pair of thin jointed legs on every segment, no eyes at all, two long feelers sweeping ahead of its head and a pair of hooked dark amber mandibles that carry a poison.

Eight rows of frames, each row a separate strip, every frame the same crawler at the same scale on the same ground line. The camera always looks down at it from about 45 degrees, so its top shows in every view:
- Row 1, four frames: the crawler scuttling toward the camera, seen from above and in front (we see its eyeless head, feelers and mandibles, with its segmented back curving up behind them; the legs ripple).
- Row 2, four frames: the crawler scuttling away from the camera, seen from above and behind (we see its segmented back of pale plates and the tip of its tail; the legs ripple).
- Row 3, four frames: the crawler scuttling to the right, in profile facing right (its whole length, the legs rippling in waves).
- Row 4, six frames: the crawler standing still, feelers twitching: two frames facing the camera, two frames seen from behind, two frames in profile facing right.
- Row 5, three frames: the crawler biting toward the camera, seen from above and in front (rears its head up, snaps its mandibles down, settles back).
- Row 6, three frames: the crawler biting away from the camera, seen from above and behind (rears its head up, snaps its mandibles down, settles back).
- Row 7, three frames: the crawler biting to the right, in profile facing right (rears its head up, snaps its mandibles down, settles back).
- Row 8, three frames: the crawler dying, seen from the front (writhes, curls up on its side, lies still with its legs folded).

The same silhouette, proportions, colours and size in every frame: only the pose and the facing change.

Hard technical requirements, these matter more than the art direction:
- Solid flat magenta background, exactly RGB(255, 0, 255), filling every pixel that is
  not part of a drawing. No gradient, no vignette, no texture, no shadow cast onto it.
- Lay the subjects out in an even grid with equal spacing. Every cell the same size.
  No labels, no captions, no text of any kind anywhere in the image.
- Every subject fully inside its own cell, centred, touching neither its neighbours nor
  the edge of the image. In an animation, the same size and the same ground line in
  every cell.
- Three-quarter top-down view: the camera looks down from the south at about 45
  degrees, so the top and the south face of things show. Light from the top left.
- Pixel art: crisp square pixels, hard edges, no anti-aliasing, no blur, no soft
  airbrush shading. Limited palette, flat shading in three or four tones, and a dark
  outline of the local colour (never pure black).
- Dark fantasy colours: muted and earthy, with one brighter accent at most. Nothing in
  a subject may be magenta, pink, purple or violet: those hues are the background key.
- They are read at 32x32 pixels in game (people at 64x64), so favour a strong
  silhouette and clear separation of parts over fine detail.
