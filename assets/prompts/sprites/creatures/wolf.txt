A pixel-art animation sprite sheet of one creature for a dark fantasy top-down role-playing game: a lean, shaggy wolf of the windy harbour island, ash-grey fur with a darker grey back and a pale grey belly, amber eyes, a long bushy tail. A hunter that stalks travellers on the coast road.

Eight rows of frames, each row a separate strip, every frame the same wolf at the same scale on the same ground line:
- Row 1, four frames: the wolf trotting straight toward the camera, head-on front view (we see its face, chest and front legs; the legs alternate).
- Row 2, four frames: the wolf trotting straight away from the camera, rear view (we see its back, hindquarters and tail; the legs alternate).
- Row 3, four frames: the wolf trotting to the right, in profile facing right.
- Row 4, six frames: the wolf standing still and breathing: two frames facing the camera, two frames seen from behind, two frames in profile facing right.
- Row 5, three frames: the wolf biting toward the camera, head-on (crouch, lunge with open jaws, recover).
- Row 6, three frames: the wolf biting away from the camera, seen from behind (crouch, lunge with open jaws, recover).
- Row 7, three frames: the wolf biting to the right, in profile facing right (crouch, lunge with open jaws, recover).
- Row 8, three frames: the wolf dying, seen from the front (staggers, collapses, lies still on its side).

The same silhouette, proportions, colours and size in every frame: only the pose and the facing change.

Hard technical requirements, these matter more than the art direction:
- Solid flat magenta background, exactly RGB(255, 0, 255), filling every pixel that is
  not part of a drawing. No gradient, no vignette, no texture, no shadow cast onto it.
- Lay the subjects out in an even grid with equal spacing. Every cell the same size.
  No labels, no captions, no text of any kind anywhere in the image.
- Every subject fully inside its own cell, centred, touching neither its neighbours nor
  the edge of the image. In an animation, the same size and the same ground line in
  every cell.
- Three-quarter top-down view: the camera looks down from the south at about 45
  degrees, so the top and the south face of things show. Light from the top left.
- Pixel art: crisp square pixels, hard edges, no anti-aliasing, no blur, no soft
  airbrush shading. Limited palette, flat shading in three or four tones, and a dark
  outline of the local colour (never pure black).
- Dark fantasy colours: muted and earthy, with one brighter accent at most. Nothing in
  a subject may be magenta, pink, purple or violet: those hues are the background key.
- They are read at 32x32 pixels in game (people at 64x64), so favour a strong
  silhouette and clear separation of parts over fine detail.
